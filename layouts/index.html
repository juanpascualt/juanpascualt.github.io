Apprenticeships and Crime:
Evidence from Spain's Vocational Training Reform

(with Daniel Montolio, Jenifer Ruiz Valenzuela)

We examine the impact of dual vocational training (DVT) on youth crime rates in Catalonia. DVT expands on-the-job training in firms relative to regular school-based vocational training. We exploit its staggered geographical rollout using a difference-in-differences approach and detailed administrative education records linked to geolocated crime data. We find that greater availability of firm-based vocational training significantly reduces local youth crime rates. Reductions concentrate during daytime hours, consistent with incapacitation. Declines are strongest for economically motivated property crimes, consistent with increased opportunity costs through improved labor-market prospects. These results suggest that apprenticeship-style programs can deliver substantial non-pecuniary social returns.

School Segregation, Educational Achievement and Sorting:
Evidence from Barcelona's Shock Plan

(with Clemens Oberhuemer, Sergi Quintana)

Barcelona's Shock Plan Against School Segregation reserves seats for socioeconomically vulnerable students in each public and charter school. We estimate impacts on school segregation, parental applications, and achievement using difference-in-differences and a structural model of school and neighborhood choice. Preliminary results show sizable reductions in segregation as schools converge to quota targets. However, higher vulnerable student shares lead to fewer applications from non-vulnerable families.
